How to think about tense

Do not choose a tense only because you see a time expression. First understand the situation and the speaker's intended meaning.

Simple

Facts, habits, states or events viewed as a whole.

Continuous

An activity in progress around a particular reference time.

Perfect

An earlier event or situation viewed in relation to a later reference point.

Perfect Continuous

An activity and its duration leading up to a reference point.

Time reference

Ask whether the speaker is looking at the present, the past or a future point.

Context matters

The same time expression can appear with different forms depending on meaning.

Learn the meaning. Understand the structure. Use it naturally.

This site uses the traditional 12-form tense-and-aspect framework common in ESL teaching. In more technical linguistic descriptions, English is often analysed as having present and past inflectional tense, with future time expressed through modal and other constructions.

Useful teaching questions

  • What time are we talking about?
  • What is the speaker focusing on: a fact, an activity, a result or duration?
  • What is the reference point?
  • Would another form change the meaning?
  • Does the sentence fit the situation?
