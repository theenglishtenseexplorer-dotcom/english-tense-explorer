START WITH THE BIG PICTURE

Time, aspect and perspective

A tense choice is not a mechanical response to a time word. It reflects how the speaker chooses to present an event or situation.

Simple aspect

Present a situation as a fact, habit, state or complete event.

Continuous aspect

View an activity as in progress around a particular reference time.

Perfect aspect

Look back from a reference point to an earlier event or situation.

Perfect continuous

Highlight an activity or its duration leading up to a reference point.

Reference time

Identify the point from which the speaker views the event: present, past or future.

Speaker's perspective

Ask what the speaker wants to foreground: fact, process, result, duration or sequence.

Understand the message first. Choose the form that expresses it.

We use the traditional 12-form tense-and-aspect framework familiar in ESL education. English also uses other constructions to express future time, including be going to, the Present Continuous and the Present Simple.
