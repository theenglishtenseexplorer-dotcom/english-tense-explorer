Get to Know Verbs

What counts as a verb—and what different kinds of verbs do.

Explore this idea

Verbs do different jobs

Action: She opened the window. Stative: I understand. Linking: The room feels quiet.

A verb's job depends on its meaning in context. Have can show possession or help form a perfect structure.

Change the Form

See how verbs shift to fit the structure and the message.

Explore this idea

Four useful forms

work · worked · worked · working

These are the base, past, past participle and -ing forms. Irregular verbs may change: write · wrote · written · writing.

The words around a verb help determine its grammatical job. Forms are not tenses.

Build with Verbs

Understand the helper verbs that hold sentences together.

Explore this idea

Meet four helping-verb groups

BE: is working · HAVE: has arrived · DO: Do you agree? · MODALS: might leave.

After a modal, use the base form: She can drive, not She can drives.

What comes next?

to + verb: decide to leave · -ing: avoid making · object + to: ask someone to help · object + base: make someone wait.

Remember to call and remember calling have different meanings.

Use verb structures to shift focus, possibility and emphasis.

One event, different structures

Active: The team completed the project. Passive: The project was completed. Conditional: If we finish, we'll call. Causative: We had it repaired.

Verb structures let speakers change focus, possibility and emphasis.
